Minus on the account: why this is the most expensive loan you have already taken

The red number on the balance sheet seems like a trifle if it does not grow. In fact, this is a daily dripping interest, often more expensive than any conventional loan. Let's look at the mechanics and real steps to stop it.

1What is a “minus” anyway?

In simple terms, you spent more than what is in your current account (חשבון עובר ושב). The bank does not block the card, but rather lends the missing amount so that the payment goes through. For this he takes a percentage - every day while the balance remains negative. In English this is called an overdraft, in Israel they usually just say “minus” (מינוס).

2Permitted and unauthorized - the difference is critical

The bank sets a limit in advance - the loan frame (מסגרת אשראי). If your minus fits in this frame, this is an allowed overdraft, the percentage is high, but fixed and understandable. If you go beyond that, that's a different story.

⚠️ Trick: the bank usually charges a noticeably higher penalty interest on the amount exceeding the limit than on the main minus within the limit. The difference can be significant - that is why it is important to know the exact size of your frame, and not approximately.

3Why is it more expensive than a regular loan?

Minus (overdraft)
The rate is tied to the current Bank of Israel key rate and can rise with it. Accrued every day while the balance is negative.
Regular loan (הלוואה)
The rate is usually fixed for the entire term at the time of registration. A clear payment schedule, you can see in advance how much you will pay in total.

That is why hanging in the red for months is almost always more expensive than taking out a regular loan once and covering the entire loss.

4What can really be done

1

Ask the bank about a minus rate reduction

The overdraft rate is not set in stone - it is negotiable. It’s not a fact that they will reduce it exactly as they promise on the Internet, but it’s definitely worth asking, especially if you are a long-time customer or have a stable income.

2

Cover the minus with a regular loan

If the minus is “stuck” on the same amount for months, it is usually more profitable to take out a targeted loan for this amount at a fixed interest rate and repay the minus in full than to continue accumulating daily interest.

3

Reduce your loan limit

After the minus is closed, ask the bank to lower your frame (מסגרת) to minimally comfortable. Less available limit means less temptation to fall through there again.

💡 Main idea: the downside is that this is not a free safety net, but the most expensive type of loan that you already have, even if you have never signed a loan agreement. It’s worth treating it that way.
