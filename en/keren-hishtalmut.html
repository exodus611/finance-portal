How does this work

The two ceilings for the self-employed are not to be confused.

Why do self-employed people have TWO different ceilings?

First - 13,203 ₪/year (4.5% of income, maximum from income 293,397 ₪) - this is the ceiling for income tax deduction: The tax is not reduced on the amount above it. Second - 20,566 ₪/year - separate, higher ceiling for exemption from capital gains tax upon withdrawal after 6 years. Many people mistakenly believe that these are the same figure - they are not, and the difference can cost thousands of shekels in lost benefits.

What is more profitable - increase to 20.566 or stop at 13.203

Adding between ₪13,203 and ₪20,566 does not give an income tax deduction, but still exempts from capital gains tax (usually 25%) when withdrawn after 6 years. For most, this is still a good deal - 0% on investment income is almost always better than paying 25% on a regular brokerage account.

Hired employee - what counts?

Deductions come from salary, but not above the ceiling 15,712 ₪/month (2026). Typically, 2.5% is withheld from you, 7.5% is added by the employer - a total of 10% of the (limited by the ceiling) salary per month, without tax on this contribution.

Benefit after 6 years - what is it?

If you don't touch the money for the first 6 years from the date of the first contribution, the entire amount - including all investment income - can be withdrawn in full without capital gains tax (usually 25% on profits on ordinary investments). This is the only instrument in Israel with this combination: growth without tax and without age restrictions.
