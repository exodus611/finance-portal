How is this even considered?

A model, not a prophecy.

Why are not payments compared, but capital?
Comparing “rent payment” with “mortgage payment” directly is incorrect: the mortgage payment partially goes into your own asset (the body of the debt), but rent does not. Therefore, it is more honest to compare the final capital at the end of the period: what happened to the real estate minus the remaining debt, versus what would have accumulated if the same amount (payment plus the difference in payments) was invested in another instrument.
Where does the “difference” that the tenant invests come from?
If the mortgage payment is higher than the rent, the tenant saves the difference and invests. If the rent is higher than the mortgage payment, the model believes that the tenant has to pay this difference out of his own money, that is, his investment portfolio grows more slowly.
Is this an accurate forecast?
No. Rising home prices, investment returns and future mortgage rates are assumptions, not facts, about the future. The model shows general mechanics and sensitivity to different bets, rather than a guaranteed result.
