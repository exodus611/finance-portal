🕯️ Yom Kippur20–21.09
Eve, 20.09 (Sunday): shortened working hours, closing ~13:00–14:00.
The day itself, evening 20.09 – evening 21.09: the whole country stops - banks, institutions, transport do not work at all, even public transport stops.
🌿 Sukkot25.09–03.10
Eve, 25.09 (Friday evening): shortened day, closing ~13:00–14:00.
Day 1, September 26 (Saturday): banks and government agencies are closed (plus it’s also a regular Saturday).
Chol Hamoed (intermediate days), 09.27–01.10: Banks work at shortened hours (usually until 12:00–13:00), Bituach Leumi is closed for personal reception, but you can resolve the issue by calling 171 (open 24 hours a day).
Hoshana Rabbah, 02.10 (7th day): usually a working day with reduced hours, many go on vacation in advance.
Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah, 03.10 (Saturday): a separate holiday immediately after Sukkot - banks and government institutions are completely closed again.
🕎 Hanukkah04–12.12
8 days, from the evening of 04.12 to the evening of 12.12 - not a day off, banks and government institutions work as usual all days, except for shortened hours on the eve (Friday).

How does the Israeli banking system work?

Prime, overdraft, Bituach Leumi - briefly about the main thing

In Israel, the banking market is based on several large players (Hapoalim, Leumi, Discount, Mizrachi-Tfahot), and rates, loans and salary deductions are called in Hebrew terms that no one explains out loud - “Prime”, “mortgage”, “Bituach Leumi”.

The Bank of Israel rate determines the “Prime” - the base for interest on almost everything: mortgages, loans, overdrafts. And the fund for advanced training (קרן השתלמות) is not a tax, but a growing savings account with a benefit after 6 years.

What does it really mean to “buy an apartment” in Israel?

Mortgage, tamhil, purchase taxes - step by step

Buying an apartment in Israel requires your own capital (usually from 25% of the cost), mortgage for the rest, and separately - taxes and expenses, which are often forgotten to be included in the budget.

Mortgage is not one credit, but a “tamhil”: a mixture of tracks with different rules (Prime, KALATZ, Mishtana). Banks usually check a “stress test” to see if the payment will be processed if Prime increases by 1%.
